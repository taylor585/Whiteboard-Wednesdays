Brief, line 2

Illustrative brief, written for this deck. Plan mode is a feature in tools such as Claude Code; in any chat you can ask for the same thing in words.

Presenter notes

About 60 seconds. Let the gold dot travel: you ask, AI asks back, a plan, you approve, and only then does it build. SAY: “The fastest way to get a bad answer is to let AI start straight away. Make it ask you questions first, then show you a plan, and wait for your OK.” In Claude Code that is plan mode; in any chat, just say it, as line 2 of the brief does.

Presenter notes

About 60 seconds. SAY: “AI has read everyone. The good and the bad. If you don’t point it at the best, you get the average.” Read the request, then the reply: it says how it will research before it does. That is line 3 of the brief.

11 / 02 / The framework · Q4

How will I know it’s good?

AI does sloppy work when nobody says what good looks like. Write the checks before it starts.

Fits three hours a week
Costs under £50
Every link works
Ends with one live campaign
Brief, line 4

A good plan fits three hours a week, costs under £50, only uses links that work and ends with one live campaign.

If it fails a check, it goes back. Not your mood: the list.

Illustrative acceptance criteria, written for this deck.

Presenter notes

About 60 seconds. SAY: “I know AI sometimes does sloppy work. So before it starts, I tell it how I will judge it.” Second click: the four checks slap onto the board. Third: “Those become acceptance criteria. If the plan fails one, it goes back. Same idea as the done gate in the AI harnesses video.”
