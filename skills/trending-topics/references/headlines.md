# Headlines

How we write every headline a Trending Topic produces: the **video title**, the **thumbnail line**, the **on-camera one-liners** ("In 5 to 10 words") and the **segment and slide headlines**. Each has a different job, and each rule below names its evidence so it can be checked.

Evidence base: the packaging research on Nick Saraev, Sabri Suby and Riley Brown (`research-findings.md` §1; full data in the thumbnail-tracker skill, `references/research/`). Figures are median outlier ratios (views ÷ median of the 10 uploads either side). Sample sizes are small, so treat them as strong signals, not laws.

## 1. The four headline types

| Type | Where it appears | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Video title | YouTube title, LinkedIn hook | Win the click from search and browse | 60 characters or fewer (the hook must survive a cut at 60) |
| Thumbnail line | On the thumbnail | Add the verdict, proof or emotion the title does not carry | 2 to 5 words |
| One-liner | READ BEFORE YOU RECORD, Act 2 cards, the whiteboard | Say what a part of the system does, out loud | 5 to 10 words, counted |
| Segment / slide headline | Act headings, Whiteboard Wednesdays slides | State one claim per segment | 3 to 6 words, ends with a full stop when it is a claim |

One promise per package: the title carries the facts, the thumbnail carries the verdict or proof, the one-liners carry the teaching.

## 2. Video titles: rules that held on all three channels

1. **Serve search and browse at once.** A searchable noun (the tool, topic or outcome people type) next to a browse hook (a test, a verdict, a clock, a stake). Both intents: 1.24× (n=33). Browse-only: 0.62×. Search-only: 0.665×. Top intent group on every channel.
2. **Name the thing early.** The tool, topic or outcome in the first 3 to 4 words. Mobile truncates around 60 characters.
3. **No plain "How to".** Plain how-to: 0.49× (n=11) in the latest 30. If it is a tutorial, frame it with a test, a clock, a count or a result.
4. **Stakes beat demos.** A challenge, a price tag or the viewer's career. Riley "We built" 4.18× (n=6) against "I built" 0.94× (n=12). Nick "What I'd Learn Instead of Automation in 2026" 26.42×.
5. **Fear works about the viewer's path, never about a model launch.** Nick's launch warnings 0.51× (n=3); his career-fear title 26.42×.
6. **Numbers only when they are the feat.** Durations, counts and scale help ("Learn 95% of Codex in 30 minutes", 6.2×). Decorative numbers and money claims do not.
7. **Put the year in evergreen titles and refresh it.** Year in title: Nick 1.51× (n=37), Sabri 1.21× (n=25), Riley 1.93× (n=7).
8. **Formulas wear out.** Borrow a creator's current formula, or an old one on a fresh topic. Never copy a formula in its tired phase (section 9).

## 3. Title formula library, matched to what Trending Topics produces

Our videos are "here is the problem, here is our system" videos built from real work. These formulas fit that shape best, in order of evidence.

| Formula | Fits a Trending Topic when | Evidence | Template | Example for our topics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stakes / multiplier | The system gives a measurable gain | Stakes beat demos (law 5) | "How {System} Can {N}x Your {Outcome}" | "How AI Harnesses Can 10x Your Quality and Output" |
| What I'd do instead | We changed how we work | Nick 26.42×, repeated at 4.72× | "Here's What I'd {Do} Instead of {Old Way} in {Year}" | "What I'd Use Instead of Prompting in 2027" |
| I tested | We actually ran it | Pooled 1.66× (n=7), most portable | "I Tested {Thing} So You Don't Have To" | "I Tested an AI Harness on a Real Client App" |
| We built | We built it with someone, on camera | Riley "We built" 4.18× (n=6) | "We Built {X} in {Time}" | "We Built a Client App While I Slept" |
| Reframe | We can flip a belief | Sabri 3.47× (n=2) | "{Hard Thing} Isn't Hard, It's Misunderstood" | "AI Isn't Dumb. It Has No Harness." |
| Time-box | We teach a broad, wanted money skill compactly | Sabri 4.68× (n=5), 4.15× on marketing/sales/ads (n=8); missed on self-help and niche tools | "Learn {N}% of {Skill} in {N} Minutes" | "Learn 90% of Meta Ads Research in 20 Minutes" |
| Secret / exposed | We show how something really works | Sabri 2024 to 25 5.35×; tired on luxury brands, fresh topic needed | "How {Industry} {Hidden Mechanism}" | "How Top Creators Pick Their Thumbnails" |
| Contrarian verdict | We have an honest negative take | Nick 6.03× (n=1) | "{Hyped Thing} Sucks, Actually" | "Vibe Coding Sucks Without a Plan" |
| Single-launch explainer | One launch this week we have used for real | Riley 5.2 to 10.8× (n=4) | "{Launch} Explained: What It Means for {Audience}" | only with Lane A proof |
| Full course | A genuinely complete course, about 2 hours or more | Nick "course" 4.15× (n=11); Riley 60+ min 2.58× | "{TOPIC} FULL COURSE {N} HOURS" | not for a 30-minute brief |

Only use a formula the video can honestly deliver: no "I tested" without a test, no time-box longer than the video, no multiplier without the numbers to back it in Act 3.

## 4. Writing the A/B set for a brief

1. Take the brief's PROMISE line and its searchable noun.
2. Draft 10 titles across at least 5 formulas from section 3.
3. Record for each: formula, character count, intent (search, browse or both).
4. Score them (section 5). Keep the top 3, each a different formula, as the A/B set in the brief's PACKAGING section.
5. Pair each title with a thumbnail line (section 6). Mark the recommended pair.

## 5. Scoring a title (0 to 100)

| Check | Points |
|---|---|
| Both intents (searchable noun + browse hook) | 25 |
| Named tool, topic or outcome in the first 4 words | 15 |
| 60 characters or fewer (or the hook survives a cut at 60) | 15 |
| Formula with evidence of 1.4× or more (section 3) | 15 |
| One promise, no stacked claims | 10 |
| The thumbnail line adds something the title does not | 10 |
| Plain words the audience would type | 10 |

Subtract 20 for a plain "How to" with no frame, 10 for a worn-out formula (section 9) and 30 for anything the video does not deliver. The score ranks a batch; it does not predict views.

## 6. Thumbnail lines

- **Always have a line.** No-line thumbnails were the weakest group on every channel (pooled 0.465×, n=10).
- **Prefer a line that adds** a verdict, proof or emotion. Sabri complements 1.49× vs repeats 0.62×; Nick reversed; Riley flat. So: prefer adding, but repeating is not fatal.
- **Show real proof when we have it** (a passing checker, a real result). Sabri's handwritten proof label 2.785× (n=6); Nick's proof screens 10.74×, 6.05×, 9.22×.
- 2 to 5 words, readable at 168 px wide.
- Hand the pair to the thumbnail-tracker skill to render and audit; this skill writes the line, not the image.

## 7. On-camera one-liners ("In 5 to 10 words")

These are what Taylor says out loud and writes on the board. `check_brief.py` enforces the count.

- Say what it does, not what it is called: "A script, not the AI, decides when it's finished." beats "The constraints done-gate."
- One idea per line. If it needs "and" twice, split the segment.
- Plain English, no jargon the audience would not search.
- Read all of them in order in READ BEFORE YOU RECORD. Together they should tell the whole story.

Worked examples from the gold brief:

| Segment | One-liner |
|---|---|
| Kickoff | AI answers its own questions first, then asks you once. |
| Five locks | Five locks keep piece twenty as good as piece one. |
| Decision tiers | Green: just do it. Amber: decide and log. Red: ask. |
| Memory | Rules and progress live in files, so nothing gets forgotten. |
| Done gate | A script, not the AI, decides when it's finished. |

## 8. Segment and slide headlines

- One claim, three to six words, a full stop when it is a claim: "Same model. Different results." / "A script decides when it's done."
- These become the Whiteboard Wednesdays slide titles when a brief is turned into a deck (hand-off in `skill-integrations.md`).
- Never a label ("Decision tiers"); always the point ("Stop asking. Never go rogue.").

## 9. Formulas to avoid right now

| Formula | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Income claims ("Generates $20K/mo") | Worn out | Nick by era 1.36× → 0.73× → 0.46× |
| 2020-style "Step-By-Step" keyword titles | Search-era format | Sabri reuse 0.56 to 0.91× |
| Launch warnings ("Do not use X") | Fear about a tool, not the viewer | Nick 0.51× (n=3) |
| Bold promises ("Everyone Who Uses This Makes $1 Million") | Unbelievable | 0.44× |
| "The one thing" | Vague | 0.71× |
| Luxury-brand "brainwash" exposés | Topic tired | Sabri latest 1.15×, remake 0.65× |
| Plain "How to" | No frame | 0.49× (n=11) |

## 10. After publishing

Ship with the top title and keep titles 2 and 3 ready. Use YouTube Test & Compare where available, or swap manually after 24 to 48 hours if click-through is below the channel norm. Keep the searchable noun constant between variants. Re-score any upload under 30 days old before learning from it.
